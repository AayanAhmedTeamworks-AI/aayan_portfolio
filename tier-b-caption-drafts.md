# `fig. ii` caption drafts

The caption slot in the figcaption sits on the **left**, with the label `fig. ii` rendering on the **right** (same layout as `fig. i` / València, MMXXV). All three options below are the left-hand text only — the `fig. ii` stays on the right regardless.

The current commit uses `—` as a placeholder. Pick one of the three, or propose your own edit.

---

### Draft A — Plain (matches "bust, 2026")

```
bust, 2026
```

Renders as: `bust, 2026          fig. ii`

Short, unadorned, matches a gallery wall card. No classical costume. Sits quietly against `fig. i — València, MMXXV`, creating an intentional register difference between the two figures (one a real photograph, one an object). My pick if you want to resist preciousness.

---

### Draft B — Latin-numeral (matches "fig. ii · MMXXVI")

```
MMXXVI
```

Renders as: `MMXXVI          fig. ii`

Single Roman year, nothing else. Harmonizes perfectly with the codex chrome. Loses one small beat of description — it relies on the image to declare itself. Strongest if the surrounding page already reads unambiguously classical.

---

### Draft C — In between

```
after a living model, MMXXVI
```

Renders as: `after a living model, MMXXVI          fig. ii`

Borrowed from museum labeling convention — classical sculpture captions frequently note `d'après nature` or `after a living model` when the subject is identified or implied. Names the relationship between you and the bust without naming you. Half-Latin, half-plain. My pick if the site leans further into the museum frame.

---

**My recommendation:** **Draft A** if you want the site to feel confident and slightly dry; **Draft C** if you want one more beat of museum register. Draft B is valid but starts to feel costume-y next to Draft A's `bust, 2026`.
